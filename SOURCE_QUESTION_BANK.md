# Core Concepts Available for Question Generation

## A. Line of Symmetry

- A line of symmetry divides a figure into two matching mirror-image halves.
- Folding can be used to test symmetry.
- A mirror can be used to test whether a proposed line is a line of symmetry.

## B. Number of Lines of Symmetry

Examples explicitly shown in the chapter include:

- Equilateral triangle — 3 lines of symmetry
- Isosceles triangle — 1 line of symmetry
- Scalene triangle — no line of symmetry
- Square — 4 lines of symmetry
- Rectangle — 2 lines of symmetry
- Capital letter H — 2 lines of symmetry
- Capital letter G — no line of symmetry

## C. Symmetry in Letters and Numbers

- Identify whether a capital letter has line symmetry.
- Count its line(s) of symmetry.
- Identify digits from 0–9 that have line symmetry in the printed style used by the textbook.

## D. Reflection

- A reflected point lies on the opposite side of the mirror line.
- The reflected point is the same perpendicular distance from the mirror line as the original point.
- Corresponding points of a shape can be reflected and joined to form the reflected image.

## E. Perspective

- Objects appear smaller as they move farther away.
- Parallel lines may appear to meet at a vanishing point in a perspective drawing.
- The vanishing point usually lies on the horizon line.
- The horizon is related to the viewer's eye level.
- In perspective, nearer objects appear larger than farther objects.
